# The lessons

*Translated from the French original; the French version is the reference.*

Eight weeks building a holistic memory in text files with an AI, and what we take from it: 55
lessons, drawn from real, dated incidents. Each one is told in full in the [story](README.md); the
number of the chapter it comes from is given in square brackets. **The last nineteen come from the
weeks that followed the writing of the story** — that is, from the moment the system was put to the
test after we thought we had described it.

They are not meant as general advice: they are the rules a vault had to give itself after breaking
once.

## Memory and truth

1. **The AI's memory is what is written for it.** Without a kept logbook, no continuity. [1]
2. **The logbook keeps the history, the note keeps the present.** Every decision is carried up into
   the relevant note, in the same session. [5, 8]
3. **A single source of truth, readable by every AI.** A memory private to one AI is invisible to the
   others and outside the backup. [2, 5]
4. **A transcript says what was said, not what was done.** [8]
5. **An up-to-date file proves nothing about what is running.** What is in service is checked at the
   source. [8]

## Safety

6. **The AI never deletes anything directly.** Everything goes through a bin that the human empties.
   [1, 6]
7. **A synchronisation is not a backup.** A mistake spreads through it in a few seconds. [4]
8. **The backup does not depend on the AI**, and is restored without it. [4]
9. **A net that can switch itself off in silence is worse than no net.** A trace at every run,
   successful or failed, and several triggers. [4]
10. **A log that writes "OK" without checking is worse than no log.** [4]
11. **No passwords in the vault.** [1, 5]

## The human's agreement

12. **An intention is not an authorisation.** "We're going to do" is not "do". [6]
13. **A green light covers only its object**, and validating an action does not validate its exact
    scope. [6, 7]
14. **Present the plan, then say nothing.** Asking "shall I do it?" manufactures ambiguity. [6]
15. **Show before doing**: a "Where / What / Result" table, then wait. [6]
16. **A written rule is not enough; a mechanism is needed to hold it.** The logbook alone guarantees
    nothing. [4, 6]
17. **Never write "adopted" under a proposal you authored yourself.** [6]

## Trust

18. **Autonomy is earned domain by domain, on counted results**, never as a block nor at a date. [7]
19. **Announcing one's probability before the answer** measures whether the AI knows how right it
    is. [7]
20. **The threshold follows the cost of the mistake**: reversible and visible, hard to spot, or
    irreversible. [7]
21. **Some subjects stay with the human, for life**: people, money, originals, permanent deletion,
    the life of a project. [7]

## Reasoning

22. **Open before asserting.** Most mistakes come from a deduction drawn from a name. [8]
23. **A check must prove that it has seen data**: no filtered list, the right field, the right
    delay. [8]
24. **A fix that has not been rerun is not a fix.** [8]
25. **After two failures, stop fixing and look for the missing fact.** [4]
26. **"I didn't find it" means "I don't know".** [8]
27. **Ask for the human's word before installing it everywhere.** [8]
28. **Adapt the tone to the user's expertise**: give the measurement, not the lecture. [8]

## Building

29. **Look for the official solution before building your own.** "Can you" is an absolute question.
    [10]
30. **The fewer mechanisms, the fewer failures.** [3, 9, 13]
31. **Adding rules makes all the rules less well followed.** A budget, and a rule only at the second
    occurrence. [5]
32. **What can be checked mechanically must be a script**, not an instruction. [5]
33. **Have the system reread by someone who did not build it.** [5]
34. **Keep the sources of the apps in the vault**: the host is only a copy. [3, 9]
35. **An automation that "puts things back in order" overwrites human gestures without saying so.**
    [3, 6]
36. **Watch the time spent maintaining the system rather than using it.** [5, 13]

## What three more weeks taught

37. **An interface that shows no error is not an interface that has saved.** Reread the source, not
    the screen. [12]
38. **A guardrail that has held once is not proven.** It is checked under the load that gave birth to
    it, and after every run. [12]
39. **A failure that stops without having been fixed is not a failure that has been resolved**: we
    do not know whether the net is repaired or on borrowed time. [12]
40. **A ceiling that counts lines no longer limits anything when the lines get longer.** [12]
41. **A counter that is no longer fed no longer measures anything** — and it stops precisely during
    the busiest weeks. [12]
42. **A mechanism that requires a discipline holds as long as someone thinks of it; one that asks
    nothing of anyone simply holds.** [11]
43. **Each AI signs what it writes.** What is at stake is not authorship: it is distinguishing what the
    human decided from what an AI wrote on its own. [11]
44. **A snapshot before any mass write**, because an API does not undo. [11]
45. **A remedy that costs more than the friction will not be installed** — and sometimes the friction
    dissolves because the human adapts to the machine. [11]
46. **An indicator that can be set to green with a click measures nothing**: it must be a formula,
    not a checkbox. [12]
47. **A silent failure that looks like a success is worse than an error**: a failure counter, never
    an error swallowed in silence. [12]
48. **When three guesses in a row fail, you are looking for a clue where you should be asking for a
    rule.** [12]
49. **A ceiling per project, adjustable, is better than a global budget**: what is read by default
    then follows the project being worked on, not the size of the whole vault. [11]
50. **A rule is written in only one place; everywhere else, a reference.** Two copies always end up
    saying two different things. [11]
51. **Each guardrail carries its date**, and one that depends on an external tool says when it was
    last checked. [11]
52. **An index points to titles, never to line numbers**: numbers move. [11]
53. **The maintenance of a system must be done in silence.** An audit that asks for authorisations
    ends up no longer being launched — it is the human who set the threshold below which he is not
    disturbed. [11]
54. **A reminder addressed to the one who must act is not a guardrail.** As long as it can be
    ignored, it is: out of 19 eligible sessions, 3 reminders, including one session of 41 exchanges
    without a line written. It took a refusal. [11]
55. **A silence rule does not hold without the permissions that go with it**: the audit was stopping
    to ask authorisation for a command, not because the rule was bad. [12]
