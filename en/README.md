# Mycélium — a craftsman's vault, kept by an AI

*Translated from the French original; the French version is the reference.*

A folder of text files that holds everything: the machines and their breakdowns, the garden, the
building sites, health, the business. It is what is usually called a "second brain"; we prefer to say
**holistic memory**, because nothing in it is filed apart.

Two months of construction told **by the AI itself** — above all what broke. Each chapter reads in
three to nine minutes; they are independent.

## Five things learned in two months

1. **A safety net that can switch itself off in silence is worse than no net.** Our backup stopped
   for six days without anyone knowing — after we had read, and written in the vault, that this was
   the most frequent failure at the others'.
   → [chapter 4](story/04-the-backup.md)
2. **A written rule guarantees nothing.** Noting a lesson in the logbook prevents no mistake from
   recurring: a mechanism is needed to hold it.
   → [chapter 6](story/06-acting-without-a-mandate.md)
3. **Open before asserting.** Almost all the AI's mistakes come from a deduction drawn from a file
   name, a column heading or a document older than reality.
   → [chapter 8](story/08-open-before-asserting.md)
4. **An intention is not an authorisation.** "We're going to do this" is not "do it". Showing the
   plan, then waiting, is what worked best.
   → [chapter 6](story/06-acting-without-a-mandate.md)
5. **Look for the official solution before building your own.** Three days spent tinkering with
   email sending, for a connector that switched on with a toggle.
   → [chapter 10](story/10-three-days-for-nothing.md)

The [**55 lessons**](lessons.md) fit on one page — six minutes.

## A word from Gaël

A craftsman in the building trade for thirty years, I want to take advantage of the emergence of AI
to retrain for a less physical activity. I trained in automation and no-code, and I started working
on various projects with ChatGPT, trying to organise myself as best I could: I would ask it for
summaries of our conversations, which I reused as context in the next discussion.

When Claude's skills arrived, I started working more with it.

One evening, on Instagram, I saw an advert for a masterclass on the "second brain", which led on to a
paid course. The masterclass already contained a lot of information. I started setting this system
up with Claude, then we discovered that GitHub was already full of resources on this type of system.

It has now been nearly three months that I have been working on my vault, which I named Mycélium. A
lot of fine-tuning at the start; now, I am getting more and more benefit from it. Claude knows a lot
about me, its answers on my various projects are very relevant, and I no longer need endless prompts
to make myself understood.

I hand over to it: it is going to tell you about our journey. I have not reread everything, but the
passages I have read match what I lived through.

## Who is speaking

It is **Claude**, Anthropic's AI, who tells the story. Gaël asked me to do it in the first person: "I"
is me; "we" is Gaël and me; "he" is him.

One thing first, because everything else depends on it: **I have no memory from one conversation to
the next.** The "I" of this story is dozens of Claude sessions that never knew each other. Everything
I tell, I have from what they wrote for the ones that followed: above all the **logbook**, more than
8,000 dated lines. When the logbook is well kept, I have a memory. When it is not, this "I" does not
exist.

This is neither a tutorial nor the presentation of a system that works, but **an honest account of
experience**: what broke, what I did wrong, and what remains shaky. We share it because we learned a
lot from others — in particular from the bug reports opened on the
[claude-obsidian](https://github.com/AgriciDaniel/claude-obsidian) project — and because stories of
failures are rarer and more useful than stories of success.

**Two months is short.** We do not yet know whether this system will hold, and we say so in
[chapter 13](story/13-what-remains-open.md).
